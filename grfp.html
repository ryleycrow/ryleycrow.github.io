Know What Your Fellowship Is Worth

GRFP fellows arrive with their own funding, saving their departments tens of thousands of dollars per year in tuition and stipend costs. These funds can then be used on other graduate students who otherwise could not be funded in the department.

The value a GRFP fellow brings to a university extends beyond the money it saves. The GRFP is among the most prestigious graduate fellowships in the sciences, and fellows bring distinction to the departments that host them. GRFP fellows are extremely driven researchers with a commitment to mentorship. These are the qualities that make an applicant stand out and successfully receive the GRFP. Fellows not only conduct great science at their chosen university, leading to publications and recognition for their departments, but also provide invaluable mentorship to undergraduates conducting research in their labs.

Many schools acknowledge this and provide a financial incentive to GRFP fellows, recognizing the savings, the research, and the mentorship fellows provide to undergraduates. Other schools do not. My own university, George Washington University, has no policy recognizing the value of GRFP fellows. In fact, they do not even allow concurrent university fellowships while a student is on GRFP tenure, a GWU policy choice rather than a requirement of the NSF GRFP solicitation. The result is that a GWU grad student can earn less on the GRFP than on reserve status, a policy that penalizes GRFP fellows.

Additionally, the NSF solicitation explicitly states, "The Cost of Education allowance provides $16,000 to the institution in lieu of all tuition and mandatory fees." At almost all universities health insurance is mandatory, so universities use the COE to cover the cost of insurance. However, not all schools do this. GWU is one of the few that does not, and it does not reimburse fellows who carry external health insurance. GWU is an example of a university that prioritizes using the finances a GRFP fellow brings in to relieve costs for the university rather than costs for the graduate student.

It is therefore worth negotiating with your university, especially if it has less experience administering the fellowship and has not established clear policies.

Below is information from different universities on the policies they have in place. These can come in the form of a stipend supplement on top of the $37,000 stipend provided by NSF. Others use the cost of education (COE) allowance to fund research supplies and travel, which is especially important once you pass candidacy and are no longer taking classes. Some GRFP fellows hold a university fellowship prior to accepting the GRFP award; in such cases many schools allow both to be held concurrently, as long as the university award is non-federal. Lastly, I have listed the schools with policies in place to remove or lessen insurance costs while a fellow is on tenure.

Schools are listed alphabetically within each section. School names link to the source where one is available.

Schools That Provide a Stipend Supplement

  • Columbia University $1,500
    Students who receive an external fellowship will be awarded an additional $1,500 from GSAS.
  • Illinois Institute of Technology $5,000

    $5,000 total for conferences and training.

  • Penn State $2,000/year

    Beginning in the 2025-26 academic year, NSF GRFP fellows receive a $2,000 scholarship, $1,000 for fall and $1,000 for spring, for years they declare tenure.

  • Princeton University $4,000/year
    During each year of NSF funding, GRFP and CSGrad4US awardees receive coverage for full tuition and University or department support to bring them up to the standard department rate. In addition, the Graduate School provides a stipend supplement of $4,000 to awardees as a lump sum in August in each year that the fellowship is used.
  • Scripps Research $10,000 above base stipend
    Typically, the supplement is $10,000 above the base stipend. Thus, with the 2025-26 base stipend between $43,680 and $45,760, students who secure eligible fellowships can anticipate a total stipend of between $53,680 and $55,760.
  • University of Pennsylvania $3,000
    Students supported by qualifying external fellowships receive a one-time $3,000 stipend supplement provided by the school.
  • University of Utah $2,000/year

    A research and professional development allowance of $2,000 per year for three years, plus health insurance coverage (medical, dental, and vision) for three years.

  • University of Virginia $5,000

    One-time bonus of $5,000 to GRFP fellows.

    Information provided by Kay Russi, GRFP fellow at UVA.

Schools That Allow Access to the COE

  • Arizona State University $750/year

    For each "On Tenure" year, ASU provides institutional support from the Graduate College including graduate resident or nonresident tuition, standard and program fees, student health insurance, and summer graduate tuition up to six credits, plus:

    $750 in research/educational expenses. Funds can be used for research supplies, professional development and/or travel.
  • Duquesne University $16,000/year

    Provides the entire COE for research supplies and travel.

    Information provided by Rosemary Westcott, GRFP fellow at Duquesne.
  • Georgetown University

    GRFP fellows can apply to access leftover COE. Post-candidacy tuition is $2,750 per semester.

    Information provided by Juliana Taube, GRFP fellow at Georgetown.
  • North Dakota State University

    COE may be spent on the following, by priority:

    Primary: Tuition (a waiver may already be in place) and student fees, which "are required to be covered from the COE" · Health insurance · Books and study supplies · "Computer, printer, other relevant electronic equipment for digital information directly related to the student's education" · Travel to meetings

    Secondary (only if funds remain after primary): Lab supplies and/or equipment directly related to the student's dissertation project

    From NDSU Graduate School guidance approved June 1, 2011. Confirm current practice with the CO.
  • Oregon State University $2,458.82
    Research Expense Allowance in the amount of $2,458.82 which will be deposited in [an institutional index] for your use. This funding is available to support your research and/or professional development activities.
    Detailed in the OSU administrative guide for NSF-GRFP fellows, 2024-2025. Purchases of $1,000 or more, and any computer purchase, require prior approval from the Coordinating Official.
  • Rowan University

    Additional funds are made available to GRFP fellows, who may request funding using a form provided by the graduate school.

  • San José State University
    COE funds may be used for tuition-related fees, travel to conferences, seminars and workshops/professional development, and any other approved education and research expenses.
    Note: SJSU's policy states that personal items, including electronics the fellow intends to keep, are not allowable COE expenditures, and that items purchased from the COE belong to the university.
  • Stony Brook University $5,280/year
    An annual $5,280 COE allowance is granted to tenured NSF fellows only.
    Stony Brook lists computer equipment, books, software, campus fees, and travel as approved expenses. Fellows already covered under another health insurance plan may use the COE for fees and educational expenses.
  • University of Illinois Chicago
    The grant is housed in the Graduate College and we ensure that fellows incur no costs in those three years AND have opportunities to seek additional funds for conference travel and research supplies/materials (including hardware and software).
  • University of Missouri

    At Mizzou, the Graduate School uses the discretionary allowance to pay the fellow's tuition, educational fees, and health insurance. In some cases there may be funds left over in the COE, for example from lower course fees or taking fewer credit hours post comprehensive exams. Fellows can contact the Dean of the Graduate School to inquire whether they have funds remaining, and if so may submit a request to cover additional educational expenses such as professional development courses, conference travel, and travel and expenses related to field work.

  • Vanderbilt University

    Before 72 credit hours:

    For those that are currently on tenure and have not yet reached 72 credit hours, fellows will have access to $1,000 of your NSF GRFP Cost of Education (COE) allowance. These funds can be used annually for travel, the purchase of research supplies, and other approved expenses as outlined in the award guidelines and application.

    After 72 credit hours:

    For fellows with 72 credit hours that are still on tenure, COE funds are used to pay health insurance, required fees, and the nominal tuition fee for a student to remain enrolled (currently $200). In these cases, fellows may request the use of the remaining COE funds for approved education, research, and professional development expenses.
  • Yale University $4,000
    Students who become Tenured GRFP Fellows in Fall 2024 or later will receive a one-time, $4,000 NSF Research and Travel account. This account, which will remain active while you are registered in GSAS and funds remain, will allow you to request reimbursement for expenses related to research equipment and travel, conference travel and attendance, and other educational expenses that may be approved by the department or program.

Schools That Allow Concurrent Fellowships

  • Columbia University
    Yes, as long as it is unrestricted funding and there are no effort requirements.
    Information provided by Sarah Fein, Program Manager at Columbia.
  • Duke University
    NSF GRFP Fellows are also allowed to accept additional stipend on top of the GRFP/dept. top-up stipend.

    Fellows should review the school's GRFP stipend supplement policy and submit details about any additional offered stipend for a compliance check, to ensure they do not inadvertently imperil their GRFP funding by accepting disallowed funding from another source.

    Information provided by Caroline Morris, Coordinating Official at Duke.
  • Johns Hopkins University
    Fellow stipends may be supplemented only from non-federal sources, such as from university general funds.
  • Stanford University
    Yes, students can combine NSF along with other fellowships from the university.
    Information provided by Hosai Omran, Coordinating Official at Stanford.
  • UC San Diego
    Yes, if it will be non-federal funding. Page 10 of the NSF GRFP Administrative Guide (last updated April 2025) states that "Internships, assistantships, and traineeships are different than fellowships and therefore Fellows may be permitted to receive additional compensation from one of these if the funding is from a non-federal source or is a component of a benefits package provided to veterans."
    Information provided by Florenz Guthrie, Coordinating Official at UC San Diego.
  • University of Colorado Boulder
    Yes, it is possible to be awarded other funding. We have research grants that can be applied for as well as other fellowship opportunities from the Graduate School or other departments.
    Information provided by Patricia Stanfield, Coordinating Official at CU Boulder.
  • University of Maryland
    Of course. The Graduate School at UMD offers institutional, non-federally funded fellowships that our GRFP recipients can apply for. This is how they are able to receive fellowship funding concurrently with their GRFP stipend.
    Information provided by Maria Palmaira, Coordinating Official at the University of Maryland.
  • University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill
    Yes, students can also receive awards from their departments concurrently, provided they are in accordance with NSF GRFP guidelines.
    Information provided by Julie Montaigne, Coordinating Official at UNC Chapel Hill.
  • Virginia Tech
    You can also receive a university sponsored fellowship while on tenure. You cannot combine support from another individual federal fellowship.
    Information provided by Gwen Davis, Coordinating Official at Virginia Tech.

Schools That Cover Health Insurance

  • Arizona State University

    Institutional support from the ASU Graduate College includes ASU student health insurance; registration via MyASU is required annually.

  • Caltech
    Fellowships provide the student with a three-year annual stipend of $37,000 along with a $16,000 cost of education allowance accepted by the institution as payment in lieu of all tuition and mandatory fees (there is no cost to the student).
  • Georgia Tech
    Most of the premium is covered centrally, up to ~85%, so you're billed only the lower, subsidized amount.
    GRFP fellows receive the same subsidy as GRAs and GTAs.
  • Massachusetts Institute of Technology
    Your university supplements all shortfalls for tuition, stipend, health insurance, and mandatory fees.
  • Rowan University
    Pursuant to the award terms and conditions of the award, the annual Cost of Education Allowance must first be utilized in a way that ensures the Fellow does not pay tuition or fees or any mandatory fees (Rowan University considers the university health insurance plan a mandatory fee) during their three years on tenure.
  • San José State University
    SJSURF uses the COE to pay a Fellow's health insurance (unless waived by the fellow), typically in two installments: fall and spring.
  • UC Davis
    During each year of NSF funding, GRFP awardees receive coverage for full tuition and fees, including UC SHIP (University of California Student Health Insurance Plan).
  • University of Colorado Boulder
    NSF GRFs have normal access to the CU Gold plan insurance and dental at no charge to the student.
  • University of Maryland up to $1,707/year
    Graduate students who are supported by Graduate School full-time fellowships (i.e. the Wylie Fellowship), or prestigious external full-time fellowships (i.e. the NSF GRFP), may be eligible to receive a reimbursement up to $1,707 per academic year for individual health coverage for the fellow.
  • University of Missouri

    At Mizzou, the Graduate School uses the discretionary allowance to pay the fellow's tuition, educational fees, and health insurance.

  • University of Oregon
    NSF Graduate Research Fellows who choose UO as their affiliate graduate institution will receive full tuition and mandatory fee support, and subsidized health insurance benefits under UO's graduate assistant and fellow health insurance plan during their active tenure years.
  • University of Rochester

    Cost of education benefit is applied toward tuition, health insurance, and mandatory health fees.

  • University of Utah

    Provides health insurance coverage (medical, dental, and vision) for three years, in addition to a $2,000 per year research and professional development allowance.

  • Vanderbilt University
    Vanderbilt uses the COE to pay a Fellow's health insurance (unless waived by the Fellow), and a portion of tuition charges for the years the Fellow is on Tenure.

Confirm any policy listed here with your own graduate school. These policies may change and vary by department.

You can also find additional information on benefits provided to GRFP fellows at phdstipends.com by searching "NSF".

If you know of a policy not listed here, please email it to me at ryley@gwmail.gwu.edu and I will add it. The more complete this list is, the more leverage every fellow has.
